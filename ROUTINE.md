# The routine — knee pushups to 30 full pushups

This is the program that ships in the app. Eight workouts: a warm-up, six push
stages, and one alternating day for pulling, legs and hips. Load them from
**Settings → Load the training program** (a fresh install already has them).

I'm not a coach or a physio. This is ordinary, conservative progression — if
something hurts in a joint-and-nerve way rather than a muscle way, especially
your back, stop and get it looked at.

## The week

Never two push days back to back. Warm-up first, every time.

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Do** | Push | Day B | rest | Push | Day B | rest / walk | rest |

Three push days a week works too (Mon/Wed/Fri) if Day B keeps getting skipped —
but don't drop Day B entirely. All pressing and no pulling is how shoulders start
aching in a couple of months.

## The push stages

You move up on a **standard, not a date**. Hit the goal written on the workout,
then switch the dropdown to the next stage. If a stage still feels rough after
three weeks, stay there — nothing is lost.

| Stage | The work | Move up when |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Knee foundation** | Toe-to-knee eccentrics 3×5, knee pushups 3×10, plank 3×30s, glute bridge, dead bug | Knee pushups are a comfortable 3×15 and planks hold 3×45s |
| **2 — Counter incline** | Counter pushups 3×10, full negatives 3×5, knee pushups 2×15, plank 3×45s, rows | Counter pushups 3×15 clean, negatives still a controlled 5 seconds |
| **3 — Chair incline** | Chair pushups 3×10, negatives 3×6, plank 3×60s, side plank, rows | Chair pushups reach 3×15 |
| **4 — First full pushups** | Full pushups 4×4 with long rests, chair pushups 2×12, negatives 2×5 | 4 sets of 8 full pushups in one session |
| **5 — Build to 15** | Full pushups 5×8, chair pushups 2×12, side planks, rows | One all-out set reaches 15 |
| **6 — 15 to 30** | Full pushups 5×12 at ~60% of your best, plus a weekly test set | The test set hits 30 |

Stage 1 is what you're already doing, plus two additions — the glute bridge and
the dead bug. That's deliberate. Everything else is the same shape, so nothing
about the first few weeks should feel like a jump.

### Why it's built this way

**Eccentrics and inclines, not more knee pushups.** Knee pushups stop carrying
you over somewhere around 15–20 reps: you get better at knee pushups and no
better at real ones. The two things that actually bridge the gap are lowering
your own bodyweight slowly (the negatives you're already doing) and raising your
hands so you press a smaller fraction of it. The incline height *is* the dial —
counter, then chair, then floor.

**The ab work is all anti-movement.** No sit-ups, no crunches, no hanging leg
raises. Planks, side planks, dead bugs and bird dogs train the core to resist
your spine moving, which is the job it actually does when you're holding a
pushup straight. They're also the ones that tend not to aggravate a cranky low
back — and the glute bridge is in there because weak hips are a common reason
the low back ends up doing work that isn't its job.

**Rows are non-negotiable.** Lie under a sturdy table, grab the edge, pull your
chest to it. It's the cheapest horizontal pull there is and it keeps your
shoulders even with all the pushing.

### Once you're at 30

Keep Stage 6 and chase 40, or make each rep harder instead of doing more: a 3-second
lowering phase, a pause at the bottom, feet elevated on a chair, then eventually
one hand further forward than the other. Rep count isn't the only direction.

## How long this takes

Honestly: **five to eight months** from knee pushups to 30 in a row, training
consistently. The first full pushup usually shows up somewhere around month two
or three. Anyone promising 30 in six weeks is selling something. The stages are
where the progress lives — the number is just what falls out at the end.

## Using it in the app

- **Warm-up** is its own workout. Run it, then start the day's session.
- The work clock is a **ceiling, not a target**. On rep work, do your reps and
  press **Set done →** — the rest timer starts immediately. Only the holds
  (plank, side plank) are meant to run to zero.
- The checklist after every set is the point: log the reps you actually did, and
  answer honestly whether your back is still fine. That last box is your early
  warning, so don't tick it out of habit.
- **Log the reps every set.** Stage 6's test set is the number you're chasing,
  and the calendar tab is where you'll see it move. Guessing from memory in
  eight weeks won't work.
- Side planks and split squats are written as "alternate sides" with double the
  sets — one set per side, swap each time.
- Adjust anything: **Edit** changes reps, times, cues and the goal line. If 90
  seconds of rest isn't enough at Stage 4, make it 150. The program is a
  starting point, not a rule.
